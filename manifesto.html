Manifesto

On clear nights east of Marfa, Texas, mysterious lights appear on the horizon. They float above the desert scrub, split in two, drift and go out. People have watched for them for more than a century, and nobody has managed to summon one. You can’t schedule them, all you can do is drive out, stand by the road and wait. Locals call these orbs the Marfa lights.

Good writing comes the same way. The right phrasing arrives from slightly off to the side. But current AI was built for the opposite. It learned to predict the likeliest next word, and is then rewarded for being agreeable and drifts toward the middle of everything ever written. What comes out is fluent and polite, but lacks substance and style.

People have started calling it slop. It’s spreading into emails, social media posts, scripts, pitches and novels, and even into AI images and video, which are often generated from descriptions written by the same models.

You can rewrite the prompt and explain exactly what you want. The model still drifts back, because the habit is in how it learned.

Marfa Labs exists to fix this problem at the root. We’re training a foundation model for one job: writing that doesn’t sound like a machine wrote it. It learns your voice and keeps it unique, so your writing never sounds like everyone else’s.
