The problem

Mode collapse,
in plain English.

Ask ten people for a story about a lighthouse and you get ten stories. Ask a fine-tuned model ten times and you get the same story wearing different hats. Post-training rewards the single most acceptable answer, and the range of plausible answers quietly collapses.

You've read the result: "delve," "tapestry," "it's not just X, it's Y," three adjectives in a row, an em dash every other line. Readers spot it in a sentence, and they stop trusting what comes after it.

  • In today's fast-paced world…
  • Let's dive in.
  • It's not just a tool, it's a movement.
  • Seamless, intuitive, and powerful.
  • I hope this email finds you well.

The method

Five layers between a blank page and a generic one.

Each layer comes from published research on preserving output diversity. We stack them, so no single fix has to carry the whole load.

  1. 01

    Annotation-anchored pre-training

    Training text is paired with labels for topic, setting, and people. Fine-tuning isn't allowed to change how the model picks those labels, so it chooses a direction first and writes around it. Reported: about 6× less diversity collapse on story writing.

    Springer et al., 2026

  2. 02

    Range-preserving fine-tuning

    GEM changes the fine-tuning objective so that learning the reference answer doesn't squeeze out the other plausible ones. Outputs get more varied at roughly the same quality.

    Li et al., 2025

  3. 03

    Preference tuning that rewards the unusual

    ReDiPO trains on pairs of good answers and marks the more unusual one as better. In the paper it was the only method where safety also improved slightly.

    Samuel et al., 2026

  4. 04

    Verbalized sampling at generation time

    Instead of asking for one answer, the agent asks for several, each with the probability the model assigns it, then deliberately draws from the tails. Reported: 1.6–2.1× more diverse creative writing with no loss in accuracy or safety.

    Zhang et al., 2025

  5. 05

    Human-first timing

    The agent doesn't write your first line. Marfa starts by asking for your raw thoughts and comes in later for structure and editing, so the direction is yours and the polish is shared.

    Marfa Labs practice

Architecture

An inspiration layer, not an autocomplete.

Younotes, voice memos, half-ideas
Inspiration graphangles, references, tensions mapped as nodes
Agent loopdraft wide, critique, de-slop, narrow
Voice memoryyour past writing, what you kept, what you cut

Today the loop runs on Claude Opus. The roadmap swaps in our own writing model, fine-tuned with layers 01–03, while the agent, graph, and memory stay the same.

Suites

One engine, tuned per format.

Long-form

Essays, newsletters, chapters. Outline from your notes, draft in your cadence, edit without flattening.

Email

Cold outreach and follow-ups that read like a person wrote them on purpose. No “I hope this finds you well.”

Marketing

Launch copy, landing pages, campaigns. Many distinct angles before one gets chosen.

Threads

Multi-post arguments with an actual through-line instead of ten numbered platitudes.

Social

Short posts across platforms, varied in shape and hook rather than one template on repeat.

Creative

Fiction and scripts, where range matters most and verbalized sampling earns its keep.
